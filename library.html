Books for the work

The syllabus.

Sometimes a book puts words to something we’ve been talking about.
These are the books I may recommend in our sessions.

You don’t need to read them all. We can pick a place to start together.

Seeing yourself clearly

Awareness

Anthony de Mello

De Mello asks you to pay attention to the beliefs and reactions you usually take for granted. He uses stories from several spiritual traditions to question what we think we know about ourselves. It’s a book you can argue with as you read.

View on Amazon ↗

Freedom and trust

Summerhill School

A. S. Neill

What happens when children have more say in their own lives? Neill describes the school he built around that question. It’s about education, but it also gets you thinking about approval, authority, and the rules you grew up with.

View on Amazon ↗

Love and relationships

A Return to Love

Marianne Williamson

Drawing on A Course in Miracles, Williamson looks at how fear shapes our relationships and how love and forgiveness might change the way we approach them. This one takes a spiritual approach to everyday problems.

View on Amazon ↗

Letting go of control

The Surrender Experiment

Michael A. Singer

Singer decided to see what happened when he stopped insisting that life follow his plan. This memoir follows that experiment through solitude, work, and building a business. It raises a useful question: how much energy goes into trying to control everything?

View on Amazon ↗

Beginning the work

The War of Art

Steven Pressfield

You know the thing you keep meaning to start? Pressfield calls the force that talks you out of it “Resistance.” These short chapters look at procrastination, self-doubt, and the habit of getting on with work that matters to you.

View on Amazon ↗

Understanding old patterns

The Body Keeps the Score

Bessel van der Kolk

Psychiatrist Bessel van der Kolk writes about how traumatic experiences can affect the body, memory, and relationships. He discusses research and different approaches to recovery. Some of the accounts are difficult to read; take your time with this one.

View on Amazon ↗

A little of my story

Grinnin’ Like a Jackass Eatin’ Briars

Jeff Batton with Landon J. Napoleon

This is my story: growing up in the South, trying to be who I thought I was supposed to be, and eventually questioning the whole arrangement. There’s some laughter in it. Some hard stuff too. It will give you a pretty good idea of who you’re talking to.

View on Amazon ↗
