Why Do I Feel So Drained in My Marriage?

By Jeff Batton · November 10, 2026

You can love somebody with your whole heart and still feel drained dry living in the same house with them.

I see that house in session more often than I can count. Two good people, neither one getting filled, both of them reaching for the other one to fix it. A man comes home hoping his wife has something to give him. A woman waits all day hoping her husband finally notices what she needs. Nobody is cruel. Nobody is lazy. They are just empty, and empty is a terrible place to try to love somebody from.

Here is how I say it when we finally get to the middle of it:

"2 empty cups in the same house together, trying to fill the other one's cup."

And here is where that road ends, every single time:

"You put two empty cups together, trying to fill the other cups, and they both drained each other even more, down to... there was nothing left to drain."

That sentence will preach all by itself, but it will not fix a marriage by itself. We have to look at why the cups went empty in the first place. Most of us were taught that a good partner gives and gives and never thinks about himself. Friend, I have watched that lesson empty more cups than any hard season ever did. You can run on shoulds for years, grinnin' like a jackass and calling it devotion, right up until there is nothing left in you to give anybody, including yourself.

And if you ask me what drains the cup the most, it is shoulding. Doing things you do not really want to do. Every time you do something you do not really want to do, you drain the cup a little more.

And here is the sneaky part: even the things that fill the cup can drain it, when you do them as a should. I rested because I should. I took time alone because I should. Same rest, same hour alone, and the cup still drains, because it was never a choice. It was one more should wearing a good costume. The filling is not in the thing. The filling is in the choosing.

Now, the first pushback is always the same one: there are things you have to do that you do not want to do. True. But you can change the way you look at them. You can choose to be present when you wash the dishes, and surrender to it, or you can be resentful and wash the dishes. Same dishes. It is a choice.

Suffering is a choice. Pain is not a choice, but suffering is a choice.

So when you stand at that sink resenting the dishes, that is shoulding. And that is what drains the cup.

Here is the part people do not expect me to say: a hard stretch in a marriage is not always a failure. Listen to this:

"Which is what relationships are designed to do. They are designed to pull this shit to the surface."

The friction is information. It shows you where you stopped taking care of your own cup and started quietly billing your partner for the filling. Once you see it that way, the work changes. It stops being about winning the next argument or fixing the other person first.

So I give the simplest homework I know:

"Keep shifting the focus back to you."

Not away from your partner in anger. Back to you in responsibility. What fills your cup? Rest, honesty, time alone, work that means something, saying no before you are already resentful. Fill it there, on purpose, and something almost unfair happens: "Rising tides lift all ships." A fuller person is easier to live with, easier to hear, and far harder to drain.

That is why I say this, and I mean it exactly as plain as it sounds:

"Inside the marriage, outside the marriage. The marriage is not the issue for me. The issue for me is you taking care of you."

You do not have to wait for your partner to change before your cup starts to fill again. Start with you. One honest look, one boundary kept, one thing that fills you put back where it belongs. Two empty cups cannot fill each other. One cup filling on purpose changes the whole house.
