Why You Keep Repeating Old Patterns—and How Awareness Helps

By Jeff Batton · September 22, 2026

Ever decide you were done with something, mean every word, and catch yourself doing it again by Tuesday?

You decided to stop saying yes when you meant no. Then somebody asked for a favor, and your mouth volunteered before the rest of you got a vote.

You meant it when you decided. You weren’t pretending. But deciding to change a pattern and noticing it while it’s happening are two different things.

That old response has had plenty of practice. It doesn’t pack its bags just because you’ve made an announcement.

Catching yourself counts

The key word is awareness.

At first, you may only see the pattern afterward. You’re driving home, replaying the conversation, and there it is: you agreed to something you didn’t want to do because disappointing somebody felt worse than disappointing yourself.

That moment matters. Even if you’ve already said yes.

Next time, you might catch it halfway through. You hear yourself explaining, apologizing, offering three alternatives nobody asked for. And you notice.

Eventually, you may catch the little pause before the answer. There’s room to ask yourself, “Do I actually want to do this?”

Same request. A little more room to choose your response.

Look at what keeps growing

You can't kill a tree by pulling the fruit off of it. It just gets more blooms and more fruit.

You can keep announcing that you’re done people-pleasing. But it helps to notice what makes that automatic yes feel necessary. What are you afraid a no would mean? That you’re selfish? Difficult? Suddenly less lovable?

You don’t have to settle all of that in one afternoon. Start by hearing the answer you usually rush past.

Try it with one ordinary moment this week. Before you agree, pause long enough to notice what you want. If you miss the moment, look back without turning it into a trial. You’re gathering information about yourself, not building a case against yourself.

Awareness doesn’t mean you’ll never repeat an old pattern. It means you can begin recognizing it sooner, and practice choosing differently.

Falling in love with yourself includes the person who notices five minutes late. That person is learning, too.
