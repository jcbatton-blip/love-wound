Why Do I Need Everyone to Understand Me? The Freedom of Not Needing Them To

By Jeff Batton · October 23, 2026

If you need everyone to understand you before you can feel okay, here is the plain answer: the understanding is not what you actually need. You need to feel secure, and you have been asking other people’s agreement to do that job. That is why the argument never ends. Even when they finally say the words, it does not hold, because agreement was never the thing you were hungry for.

I say this in session after session, and it stops people every time:

That unconditional love is freedom, because I don’t care whether you get it or not. I don’t need you to get it.

The shower conversations give it away

I call them the shower conversations. You are standing in the shower, arguing with somebody who is not there. I wish I could tell them. I wish they could just see. You rehearse your case, you win the argument, and you step out feeling worse than when you stepped in.

That rehearsal is the tell. A person who is free does not need to win arguments against people who are not in the room.

It’s the need for their response that is the indicator.

Not the strength of your point. Not how wrong they are. The need itself. How badly you need them to see it your way is a measure of the insecurity underneath, the part of you that still needs to be validated before it can rest.

The issue is never the issue

Watch what happens in a marriage, or with an adult child, or at work. The fight is about the house, the money, the plan, the tone of one text message. It is never about that. The issue is never the issue. Underneath it is an old story, usually installed in childhood, that says your worth depends on being understood, agreed with, and chosen. So every disagreement feels like a verdict on you.

You cannot fix that by explaining better. You have already explained it perfectly, in the shower, a hundred times.

What unconditional love actually means here

Unconditional love is not a feeling you wait for. It is what happens when your love, for yourself first and then for them, stops carrying conditions. If you cannot love yourself unconditionally, it is going to be really hard to love anyone else that way. Love that only flows toward people who return it the way you want is a transaction. What does it profit you to love those who love you exactly right?

Freedom sounds like this, said plainly and meant: I love you, I see it differently, and I do not need you to get it. And the strange thing is, the understanding you kept chasing tends to show up on its own, later, once you no longer need it.

You do not need them to get it. That is the freedom.

Start small this week. Notice one shower conversation and put it down mid-sentence. Then say the true thing once, kindly, to the actual person, and let their response be theirs. Their reaction is not your work. Your freedom is.
