Why Do I Feel Like I Need My Partner to Make Me Okay?

By Jeff Batton · October 29, 2026

Because somewhere along the way, your partner got a job they never applied for. The job is making you feel okay. When they do it well, you feel loved. When they have a bad day, a quiet mood, or a life of their own, you feel abandoned. That is not a partner problem. That is what need does to love. Need turns a person into a supplier, and then it grades them on delivery.

Need is a performance contract

Here is how I say it in session:

Most couples are doing the exact opposite. I need you to perform in such a way so I can feel something.

Read that slowly. I need you to perform. Your mood, your words, your attention, all of it becomes the evidence I use to decide whether I am okay today. If you perform well, I am fine. If you do not, I am hurt, and it is your fault.

Nobody can carry that job for long. The person carrying it starts to feel managed instead of loved. The person assigning it never feels secure, because their okay is always in somebody else’s hands. Both people end up tired, and neither one can say why, because on paper nothing is wrong.

The biggest lesson here is simple: you don’t need anybody to do anything.

That sentence is not cold. It is the door.

The cup and the button

Think of yourself as a cup. Filling it is your job. Most of us get our cup about halfway full and then hand it to our partner. Fill the rest, we say. Make me feel wanted, settled, okay. They can’t. Not because they don’t love you. Because it isn’t their cup. And when the level stays at halfway, we call them the problem, when the truth is we stopped pouring.

Underneath it sits a quieter demand: I need you to be a certain way so I don’t feel something. Don’t be quiet, or I feel ignored. Don’t disagree, or I feel unsafe. But feeling those things is not the failure of a relationship. It is the material of one. The work is to feel it, recognize it, and realize it has almost nothing to do with the other person.

They just pushed the button. The button was already there. Touch a dog’s broken leg and the dog bites. The bite is not about your hand. It is about the leg. Take the wound away and the same touch is just a touch. Most of the time your partner is simply being themselves, living their own day. They are not out to get you. They bumped into a place in you that was already hurt, and the hurt answered before you could.

So the question changes. Not, why did you push my button. But, why is the button there, and what would it take to heal the leg. That question belongs to you. It is also the most loving question you can bring into a marriage, because it stops billing your partner for your own wound.

The freedom formula

Real love sounds like this:

I don’t need you, I get to be with you. I don’t need to be with you, I get to be with you. And if you stay, great. And if you don’t, great.

You would be sad if they left. Sad is honest. Your world would not be over. That is the difference. Need says, I cannot be okay unless you do this. Freedom says, I am okay, and I choose you from that place.

From need, every request is a test. From freedom, a request is just a request. From need, a partner’s quiet evening is a threat. From freedom, it is just a quiet evening. The relationship did not change. The place you are standing changed, and everything you see from there changed with it.

Same partner. Different place to stand. Different marriage.

How to start

First, catch the contract. The next time your mood drops because your partner did not perform, say it plainly to yourself: I just handed them my okay. Naming it takes the job back.

Second, do your own okay first. Before you ask them to fix the feeling, ask what you actually need, and give yourself the first honest piece of it. Rest, air, quiet, a walk. You will be surprised how many emergencies were just being tired, talking loud.

Third, practice the sentence until it sounds like you. I don’t need you. I get to be with you. Say it on an ordinary day, not in a fight. It is not a speech for them. It is a new place for you to stand.

If your whole day rises and falls on how one person treats you, we should talk. This is the work I do every day: handing people their own okay back, so love can be a choice again instead of a job.
