Why Do I Feel Like I’m Not Enough, No Matter How Hard I Work?

By Jeff Batton · November 7, 2026

If you feel like you are not enough, more effort is not the cure. I know that sounds backwards. When money is tight and life feels small, working harder is the one lever most of us know how to pull. But if the thing driving you is a sentence you believe about yourself, harder work just shakes the snow globe faster. The view gets worse, not better. The fix is quieter than you think. You set the globe down, and you deal with the sentence.

A client who worked all the time

A woman I work with is good at what she does. Clients call her. Her calendar is full. She works hard, and she works a lot. By every measure the world uses, she should be fine.

Last week she paid her bills, opened her bank account, and stared at what was left. It was barely grocery money.

She said what a lot of people say at that moment. I just need to work harder. I need more clients. I need to run faster.

I have sat across from that sentence for years. So I told her the truth I have learned to say plainly. You cannot run fast enough to get this. And you cannot shake a snow globe hard enough to see what is inside it.

Shaking harder is not a strategy

Picture the globe. You want to see the little house inside it clearly, so you give it a good shake. The snow flies everywhere, and now you can see even less than you could before.

That is what most of us do with our lives. We rearrange. We hustle. We add one more thing and call the blizzard progress. Then we wonder why we are exhausted and still cannot see where we are going.

I have met very few people whose real problem was a lazy streak. Most of the people who come to me are not lazy. They are tired. There is a difference. You do not fix tired by running faster.

The sentence running the show

Her problem was never her schedule. It was a sentence she has believed for years, so quietly that she forgot it was a sentence at all.

I am not enough.

Watch how that sentence runs a life. You put yourself at the end of your own list, every time. A client asks you to move a meeting up an hour, and you hand over your morning without a thought. You skip the shower. You stay in your pajamas and run from the minute you wake up until the minute you fall asleep. Keeping everybody else happy feels more important than your own day, because somewhere underneath, you believe that if you stop running, people will stop loving you.

And the money just tells the same story in numbers. Scarcity is familiar. It fits the sentence. A full calendar and an empty bank account can live in the same life when the belief underneath them never changes.

This is why the feeling survives success. A raise, a good month, a compliment. The snow settles for an afternoon, and then something shakes the globe again, and you are right back to proving. The proof never holds, because the sentence was never about the evidence.

Nobody is grading you but you

Here is the part that surprises people. Nobody else can see that sentence but you. Nobody is walking around grading your worth. Nobody is keeping score at the grocery store.

The world is mostly reflecting back to you what you already believe about yourself. That is good news, when you sit with it. The grade you keep trying to earn is one you made up, and what you made up, you can set down.

You can say "I am enough" until the cows come home. The words are fine. They are just not the work. The work is learning to live from it before you fully feel it. Act like it, until you believe it, until you start to see it.

What setting the globe down looks like

It looks smaller than you would expect. It looks like one honest no. No, I cannot move that meeting. We will have to reschedule. It looks like getting dressed for your own day before you serve anybody else’s. It looks like putting your name at the top of your own list for once, not as a reward for finishing, but as the starting point.

The snow does not settle because you shook harder. It settles because you set the globe down.

You are enough. You were never the problem to solve. The sentence was. And a sentence, once you can hear it, is something you can finally stop obeying.
