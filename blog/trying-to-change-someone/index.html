Why You Keep Trying to Change Someone—and What to Notice

By Jeff Batton · September 24, 2026

Ever catch yourself explaining the same thing to the same person, only louder this time?

Surely they understand by now. You’ve used examples. You’ve been patient. You may have prepared a closing argument that would impress a jury.

And here you are again.

Mad at the turtle

Picture somebody standing beside a tree, furious because a turtle won’t climb it.

The squirrels manage. So do the cats. Our friend has explained the assignment. The turtle remains unimpressed.

From a distance, it’s funny. Up close, it can feel an awful lot like a relationship.

You keep asking someone to become the person you believe they could be. You can see it so clearly. If they would just listen, just try, just understand this one thing, everything could be different.

Sometimes people do change. Sometimes a clear request opens a conversation you haven’t had before. People are more complicated than turtles, thank goodness.

But what happens when you’ve had the conversation, and the answer keeps arriving in what they do?

You can spend a lot of love arguing with what’s in front of you.

That doesn’t mean your needs are too much. Wanting kindness, honesty, or a little help carrying the groceries is hardly an extravagant personality flaw.

It means you might need to look at what’s actually available, rather than what you keep hoping another explanation will produce.

Listen to the answer you’re getting

This is where awareness gets uncomfortable.

You may notice that you’ve been waiting for somebody else to change before allowing yourself to make a decision. While you’re busy explaining, you don’t have to ask what you want to do if things stay as they are.

That’s a harder conversation. It’s also one you can have with yourself.

Try writing down one request you’ve made repeatedly. Beside it, write what has actually happened. Leave out the reasons you imagine, the promises you’re counting on, and the speech you’re planning for Thursday.

Just look at the two lines.

You don’t have to decide the whole future today. You might make a clearer request, set a limit, or stop taking responsibility for someone else’s part.

Falling in love with yourself includes taking your own needs seriously enough to hear the answer, even when it isn’t the one you wanted.
