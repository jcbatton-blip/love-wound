Making the unconscious conscious

Freedom begins
with a different
choice.

You can know better and still do the same old thing.

Say yes when you mean no. Take care of everybody, then resent that nobody takes care of you. Find yourself in the same relationship with a different person.

Most of us learned what love, success, and responsibility looked like before we were old enough to question it. We absorbed our families’ expectations and childhood lessons. Some of those instructions are still running the show.

We talk about the thing that keeps happening. I listen, ask questions, and hold up a mirror. When you can see the old script, you have somewhere new to begin.

You’re not stuck. You’re running a play you learned years ago. Once you see it, you can begin to practice a different response.

The patterns we bring to love

“Your insecurities
are fighting.”

You start with the dishes. Somehow, you’re arguing about the whole marriage.

Sometimes the argument carries an old love script: what you learned you had to do to be heard, stay safe, or feel wanted. One person pushes. The other pulls away. Both feel misunderstood.

I call it the love wound. What’s familiar can feel like love—even when it hurts. We can find ourselves reenacting the family patterns we hoped to leave behind.

We slow the conversation down. What happened? What did you hear? What were you afraid it meant? Seeing the pattern gives you a chance to hear the person in front of you.

Awareness is the beginning. Practice is the work.

Awareness changes the choice.
Choice changes the result.

01 / NOTICE

Catch the old play.

At first, you notice afterward. Then halfway through. Eventually, you hear the familiar response before it leaves your mouth.

02 / CHOOSE

Try another response.

Pause. Ask what they meant. Say what you need. We work with the ordinary moments where a different choice becomes possible.

03 / PRACTICE

Run it again.

Like learning a play on the court, you repeat it until the new response becomes second nature. Catching yourself counts. You keep practicing.

Hey. I’m Jeff.

I spent years
trying to fix myself.

I read. Studied. Prayed. Searched. Analyzed. My own Don Quixote quest, chasing every windmill I could find.

Then I realized: believing I was broken was part of the trap.

That became the foundation of my work. For thirty years, I’ve sat with people, listening for the things we learn to do to be loved—and what happens when we keep doing them long after they’ve stopped helping.

Most of my day, I spend talking people into loving themselves and having fun. You’d think those two things would come naturally. We can make them awfully complicated.

You can expect questions, straight talk, and room to disagree with me. And yes, we can laugh. A freer life ought to have some joy in it.

Before we meet

A few things
you might wonder.

Can we talk before I book a session?

Yes. Start with a free discovery conversation. Ask questions, get a feel for me, and decide whether we’re a fit. Book a free call.

Is this therapy?

No. This is life coaching. We explore what’s happening in your life and practice how you want to respond. I don’t diagnose or treat mental health conditions.

How long does change take?

There isn’t a fixed timetable. Seeing a pattern is a beginning. Trust, repetition, and practicing a different response take time. We decide together what continuing the work could look like.

Where do we meet?

Individual and couples sessions take place online. Weekend details are arranged together before booking.

Do I need to prepare?

Bring whatever keeps coming up. You don’t need a tidy explanation. If reading helps, you can explore the books I return to.
